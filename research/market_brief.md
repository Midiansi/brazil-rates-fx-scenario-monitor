# Brazil macro: carry, the election and one conditional trade

*Research brief · thesis dated 24 September 2026. Educational research, not investment advice.*

## My current view — No position

The real is being held up by its large interest-rate cushion. The next big move depends on the 4 October election — which I can't forecast — so I'm waiting for the market to show its hand.

1. **The cushion is still large.** Even after Brazil cut to 13.75% and the U.S. raised rates to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September, Brazil's policy rate is 9.88 pp higher. Holding reais instead of dollars pays roughly 0.76% more per month — traders call this carry.
2. **Investors are charging for risk.** Brazil's two-year market rate is 13.76%, about 1.5 pp above the average implied by economists' own rate forecasts (12.29%). I read much of that gap as a premium for fiscal and political uncertainty.
3. **The election is too close to call.** Datafolha has Lula at 39% and Flávio Bolsonaro at 36% for the first round (margin ±2 pts). The 2027 budget is disputed: the government projects a R$18.6 bn surplus, the Senate's fiscal watchdog a R$86.1 bn deficit.

**What would make me act.** After the first round: two daily PTAX closes below 5.10 while the U.S. two-year yield stays at or below 5.00%. I would read that as investors seeing less fiscal risk, and paper-buy the real.

**What would change my mind.** A PTAX close above 5.30 before any entry, Brazil's two-year rate above 14%, or the U.S. two-year yield above 5.10%. Each would mean risk is rising, not falling.

## What happened to my 13 September thesis

On 13 September I expected both central banks to deliver what markets priced, and planned to buy dollars only if USD/BRL broke above 5.22. No position was ever opened, so there is no performance to report — only reasoning to check.

| What I expected | What happened | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Copom cuts 0.25 pp; the Fed raises 0.25 pp. | Both on 16 September: Selic 14.00% → 13.75%; Fed 3.50–3.75% → 3.75–4.00%, unanimously. | Confirmed |
| Brazil's rate lead narrows by about 0.50 pp. | 10.38 pp → 9.88 pp. | Confirmed |

| Oil above $100 supports the real. | Brent spot peaked at $130.80 on 15 September, yet the real weakened. Oil may have cushioned the move; the data cannot separate it from U.S. rates and politics. | Unresolved |

**What I changed.** The size of the rate cushion mattered more than its 0.50-point change, and I treated the election as a footnote when it is the main event. The new thesis starts from those two corrections.

## The paper trade: buy the real only if the market confirms

Inactive · no position · earliest entry 5 October

- **Expression.** Long the Brazilian real against the U.S. dollar (short USD/BRL), tracked with the official PTAX rate. Paper only: no money, no execution.
- **Thesis.** If the election lowers the fiscal risk premium, the real should strengthen and stay stronger, because investors keep collecting a large interest-rate cushion while they wait.
- **Why it may not be priced.** The two-year rate sits 1.5 pp above the economists' own path, and economists expect USD/BRL at 5.20 at year-end. A durably stronger real is not the consensus, and the premium could compress quickly if uncertainty clears.
- **Catalysts.** First round 4 October; possible runoff 25 October; Fed 28 October; Copom 4 November.
- **Entry.** From 5 October: two consecutive daily PTAX closes below 5.10, with the U.S. two-year yield at or below 5.00%. 5.10 is near the bottom of the last 20 closes (5.0853–5.2002).
- **Drop the idea (before entry).** Any PTAX close above 5.30 — higher than every close of the past four months (peak 5.2233 on 14 August). The idea is dropped, not reversed.
- **Exit (after entry).** Two consecutive closes above 5.20 — the top of the recent range and the economists' year-end median — or the U.S. two-year yield above 5.10%.
- **Review.** Around 4.97–5.00 (range low minus range width), or on 4 November at the latest. A review zone, not a target.

## What could happen after 4 October

- **A. Risk premium falls** — PTAX closes below 5.10 twice; Brazil's two-year rate falls a quarter point or more from 13.76%; implied inflation eases. Investors see less fiscal risk, and the carry cushion keeps attracting money. *Paper-buy the real if the U.S. two-year yield is at or below 5.00%.*
- **B. No verdict until the runoff** — PTAX stays between 5.10 and 5.30; polls remain tight. The market waits for 25 October. *No position. Reassess after the runoff and the 4 November Copom decision.*
- **C. Risk premium rises** — PTAX closes above 5.30; the two-year rate moves above 14%; implied inflation rises. Fiscal worries dominate. A weaker real plus expensive oil could make the Copom pause its cuts. *Drop the long-real idea. A dollar trade would need new rules; I would not recycle September's.*
- **D. Outside shock: U.S. rates** — The U.S. two-year yield rises above 5.10%, or the ten-year jumps further. Emerging-market currencies tend to weaken together, whatever Brazil's election says. *No long-real position; exit if already in one.*

## The evidence, sorted by type

**Fact**
- The Copom cut the Selic by 0.25 pp to 13.75% on 16 September, unanimously — its 5th cut in a row and 1.25 pp since March. It gave no guidance on the next step, said inflation risks are tilted upward and projects 3.2% inflation in early 2028, still above the 3% target.
- The Fed raised its target range by 0.25 pp to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September (12–0), saying inflation “remains elevated”.
- The gap between the two policy rates fell from 10.38 pp to 9.88 pp.
- Brazil votes on 4 October; a runoff, if needed, is on 25 October. Datafolha (15–17 Sep): Lula 39%, Flávio Bolsonaro 36% in the first round; 46% vs 44% in a runoff (±2 pts).
- For 2027 the government's budget bill projects a R$18.6 bn primary surplus; the Senate's Independent Fiscal Institution projects a R$86.1 bn deficit.

**Market pricing**
- USD/BRL (PTAX) was 5.1792 on 24 September, +1.7% since 11 September but still inside its four-month range (5.0069–5.2233).
- The U.S. two-year Treasury yield rose from 4.56% (10 Sep) to 4.87%; the ten-year is 5.18%.
- Brazil's fixed-rate curve (ANBIMA, 23 Sep): one year 13.47%, two years 13.76%. Inflation implied by the curve over two years: 5.9%.
- Brent: the EIA spot price was $114.89 on 22 September after peaking at $130.80 on 15 September (it was $85.51 in late July). The November futures contract usually quoted in the news closed at $98.12 on 23 September.

**Survey & projections**
- Focus survey medians (18 September): Selic 13.50% at end-2026, 12.00% at end-2027; inflation 4.9% in 2026, 4.3% in 2027; USD/BRL 5.20 at end-2026.
- Fed officials' median projection: 4.1% at end-2026 — one more 0.25 pp rise — up from 3.8% in June.
- The EIA (9 Sep) expected Brent to average about $90 in the second half of 2026, before the 11 September attack on Saudi Arabia's East–West pipeline.

**My interpretation**
- If policy follows the economists' path, the average overnight rate over the next two years would be about 12.29%; the market's two-year rate is 13.76%. The 1.5 pp difference is either a view that the Copom will cut less, or a premium for risk. I read most of it as risk premium because implied inflation (5.9%) sits far above forecasts (4.3% for 2027) — but the data cannot prove that split.
- The wide gap between spot and futures oil prices says physical supply is scarce now but the market expects it to ease. Oil's support for the real may therefore be temporary.
- A 0.50-point narrowing of a 9.9-point gap barely changes the incentive to hold reais; a change in the risk premium can move the currency much more.
