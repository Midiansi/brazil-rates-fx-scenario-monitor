# Brazil macro: the market paid for the first round. Will it keep paying?

*Research brief · thesis dated 6 October 2026. Educational research, not investment advice.*

## My current view — No position

The market paid for Flávio Bolsonaro’s lead in one session: the dollar fell 4.55% against the real and the two-year rate fell 111 basis points. I stay out until 25 October, because my entry rule has become a test of whether that payment is kept.

- **USD/BRL (PTAX):** 4.9856 (−4.55% · 5.2235 on 2 October)
- **Two-year zero rate (ANBIMA):** 12.60% (−111 bp · from 13.71%)
- **Market minus economists, two years:** +0.35 pp (from 1.46 pp)
- **Ibovespa (press):** +7.7% (record close, 206,912 points)

1. **It was the largest first Monday in seven elections.** USD/BRL fell 4.55% to 4.9856 on 5 October, 8.1 times the previous month’s daily volatility, against 2.9%–3.8% after the 2014, 2018 and 2022 first rounds. It is the 8th-largest one-day gain of the real since 2002.
2. **The curve moved further than the currency.** The two-year zero rate fell 111 bp to 12.60% and the five-year 137 bp; 61% of the two-year fall is lower implied inflation. The premium over the economists’ own rate path went from 1.46 pp to +0.35 pp: it compressed on the first round, not after the runoff.
3. **In past elections the vote, not the first Monday, set the level.** By the Friday before the runoff the real had given back 85% (2014) and 70% (2022) of its first-Monday gain; in 2018 it kept going. After the runoff the three paths split again, from a full reversal in 2014 to a further rally in 2018.

**What would make me act.** After the runoff, from 26 October: two daily PTAX closes below 5.10 with the U.S. two-year yield at or below 5.00%. With the real at 4.9856, that now asks only that less than 48% of Monday’s move be given back, so a trigger would tell me little: I would read it as a prompt to review, not a green light. Earlier closes do not count.

**What would change my mind.** A PTAX close above 5.30 before any entry, Brazil’s two-year rate above 14%, or the U.S. two-year yield above 5.10%. A PTAX at 5.22 alone would mean 99% of the first-Monday move undone. Each would mean risk is rising, not falling.

**What I changed.** I sized the first Monday from three elections; it was larger than all of them, and it came with the whole curve, not only the currency. I have not moved a single level after the fact: a rule rewritten after the move is no longer a pre-commitment. What changed is how I read it. The entry level used to ask whether the market would pay for fiscal credibility; it now asks whether the market keeps what it has already paid.

## Rates, FX and quant: pricing the first Monday

Three questions a rates and FX desk would put to a 4.55% move: how did the curve reprice, is a first-Monday move a level shift or a transient, and what does my own rule now measure?

**The curve repriced more than the currency.** The front end, anchored by the Selic (13.75%), barely moved; the fall grew with maturity, from 33 bp at six months to 111 bp at two years and 137 bp at five, so the curve now slopes down to the two-year point and back up to the ten-year. Against the economists’ own Selic path it is −0.31 pp at one year but still 1.01 pp higher in the second year (1y1y forward 12.52% against 11.51%): the market pulled the cuts forward but does not expect them to go as deep. About 61% of the two-year fall is lower implied inflation (5.91% → 5.27%), the rest lower real rates (7.37% → 6.97%). The survey is a week old (the Focus of 2 October predates the vote), so part of the gap will close from the economists’ side.

**Seven first rounds, one outsized Monday.** I measured the first business day after each first round since 2002 against the 20 sessions before it. Only three earlier Mondays were surprises, moving USD/BRL by more than twice its usual daily volatility: 2014, 2018 and 2022. Before the runoff the real gave back most of the move in 2014 and 2022 and extended it in 2018; after the runoff the paths split again, from a full reversal in 2014 to a further rally in 2018. The 2026 Monday (4.55%, 8.1 σ) is the largest of the seven. Three cases are a base rate, not a test, but they say the first Monday has not predicted where the real ends up: the vote has.

**My own rule, restated in the units of the move.** The levels I wrote on 5 October were set for a real at 5.2235. A 4.55% fall has carried the real through them, so each level now says something different. I express them as the share of Monday’s move that the real would have to give back, and compare each with a random walk from today’s close.

- **Benchmark.** A random walk from 4.9856 with the pre-vote volatility (9.1% a year) and a runoff-day shock taken from past runoffs (1.4% root mean square) would close below 5.10 on 26 October in about 82% of paths, and in about 74% with long-run volatility (14.4%). At the first post-runoff close, the three surprise years kept more than half of Monday’s move in two cases out of three (2018 and 2022). The entry rule is no longer selective: a trigger tells me little, and a miss tells me the real has given back more than 48% of its move.
- **Volatility.** Realised volatility over the last 20 sessions is 18.8% a year, but 72% of the variance is the single jump on 5 October. The jump-robust (bipower) estimate is 10.0%; the long-run level since 2003 is 14.4%. I use 9.1% for the diffusion and treat the jump separately; the 5 October note used the 20 sessions up to 2 October.
- **Carry.** The Selic–Fed gap earns 0.76% a month (9.5% a year) through forwards, not through PTAX. Against 9.1% diffusion volatility that is a carry-to-volatility ratio of 1.0; against long-run volatility, 0.7.
- **If it fires.** If the rule fires near today’s level, the position starts inside my own review zone: the levels were derived on 2 October from the 20 sessions to that day (5.0853–5.2235). I keep them, because a level rewritten after the move is no longer a pre-commitment, and I treat a trigger as a prompt to review. The distance to JPMorgan’s favourable case (4.90) is 1.7%; to my exit level, 4.7% the other way.

## Commodities: Brazil sells dollars, and the real prices them

Oil, soybeans and iron ore are 34% of Brazil’s goods exports; with coffee, sugar and maize, the six benchmarks I can measure cover 44% (UN Comtrade, 2025). Two questions: how much of the real do they explain, and what does a 4.55% move in the real do to the price a producer actually receives?

**How much of the real do commodities explain?** I regressed monthly changes in USD/BRL on monthly changes in a six-commodity basket weighted by 2025 export values (258 months, Jan 2005 to Jul 2026). The basket explains 28% of the variance, with a slope of −0.40: a 10% rise in the basket has gone with a real about 4.0% stronger. The fit is stable across the two halves of the sample (34% in 2005–2014, 24% in 2015–2026). So commodities matter, but 72% of the real’s monthly variance comes from rates, fiscal risk and politics. The first Monday of October was the second kind: USD/BRL fell 4.55% even as Brent fell 1.9%.

**What producers see: dollars versus reais.** A producer sells in dollars and pays in reais, so the price that matters is the dollar price times USD/BRL. Over the twelve months to Jul 2026 USD/BRL fell 7.5% on average, which took about seven to nine points off the reais return of every benchmark. The real is a partial natural hedge: its monthly moves run against commodity prices (oil −0.43), which trims the volatility of the reais price by up to 10% for oil and 3% for soybeans, and by nothing for iron ore, coffee or maize. On 5 October the hedge did not work: the real strengthened while oil fell, so every dollar-priced export lost 4.55% in reais at unchanged dollar prices.

- **Spot is not futures.** EIA’s spot series (dated Brent) was $113.96 on 29 September, against a peak of $130.80 on 15 September. EIA notes that in September spot ran well above the Brent future (about $132 against $109 at their peaks) because supply disruptions left buyers short of physical barrels. I read the spot series as a gauge of physical tightness, not as the price a hedger faces.
- **Why Brazil cares.** Crude is Brazil’s largest export (12.8% of 2025 goods exports), so the oil price supports the external accounts and the real. It also feeds inflation (Focus expects 5.0% for 2026, above the 4.5% ceiling of the target band) and keeps the Copom cautious. A reopening of the Strait of Hormuz would remove both supports at once.
- **A price view is not a company view.** On 5 October Petrobras shares rose 8.2% while Brent fell 1.9%: an equity move on governance and fiscal risk, not on oil. The commodity and the state-controlled company are different trades.

**Sugar, ethanol and the real.** Sugar is priced in dollars; ethanol in reais, against gasoline. A stronger real cuts the reais price of sugar by the same 4.55% (down 16.3% over twelve months, against 9.5% in dollars), and expensive oil makes ethanol more valuable: both push mills toward ethanol. Industry projections made before September’s oil spike had the 2026/27 mix near 47% sugar and ethanol at a 61% parity with gasoline, favourable to flex-fuel drivers; Hedgepoint saw a sugar floor near 14.2 US¢/lb, where ethanol gains competitiveness, and the IMF’s July average (14.81) sat just above it. Less sugar at the margin would support New York prices. The mix is a monthly decision and my data are not: this is a mechanism to check against UNICA’s crush reports, not a call.

## The paper trade: wait for the runoff, then act only if the market keeps what it has paid

Inactive, no position · earliest entry 26 October

- **Expression.** Long the Brazilian real against the U.S. dollar (short USD/BRL), tracked with the official PTAX rate. Paper only: no money, no execution.
- **Thesis.** If the election lowers the fiscal risk premium, the real should strengthen and stay stronger, because investors keep collecting a large interest-rate cushion while they wait.
- **What is already priced.** The first Monday took the real to 4.9856 and the two-year rate to 12.60%. The two-year rate is now +0.35 pp against the economists’ own path, and the economists’ year-end median for USD/BRL (5.20) predates the vote. JPMorgan’s favourable case (4.90) is 1.7% below today’s close.
- **Catalysts.** Runoff 25 October; Fed 28 October; Copom 4 November.
- **Entry.** From 26 October, the first PTAX after the runoff: two consecutive daily closes below 5.10, with the U.S. two-year yield at or below 5.00%. Earlier closes, including the rally of 5 October, do not count.
- **Drop the idea (before entry).** Any PTAX close above 5.30, higher than every close of the past four months (peak 5.2235 on 2 October). The idea is dropped, not reversed.
- **Exit (after entry).** Two consecutive closes above 5.22 (the top of the pre-vote range), or the U.S. two-year yield above 5.10%.
- **Review.** Around 4.94–5.00 (pre-vote range low minus range width), or on 4 November at the latest. A review zone, not a target.
- **How the rule reads today.** The real is at 4.9856, 2.3% below the entry level: the entry condition is met in price but not in time, and the date clause is all that separates the rule from a signal. The levels date from 2 October; I have not moved them after the vote.

## What could happen before and after 25 October

- **A. Monday’s move is kept** — PTAX stays below 5.10 through 25 October (less than 48% of the move given back); the two-year rate holds near 12.60%; the winner shows a credible fiscal path. The market keeps paying for fiscal credibility. Little is left to buy: I would be entering inside my own review zone. *The rule can fire from 26 October if the U.S. two-year yield is at or below 5.00%. I treat it as a review, not a green light.*
- **B. The rally fades** — PTAX gives back more than 48% of the move (back above 5.10) before the vote or on 26 October; polls tighten. The market doubts the first-round signal, as it did before the 2014 and 2022 runoffs. *No position. Wait for the next confirmation.*
- **C. The premium returns** — PTAX closes above 5.30 (132% of the move given back); the two-year rate moves above 14%; implied inflation climbs back toward 5.91%. Fiscal worries dominate again; a weaker real plus expensive oil could make the Copom pause its cuts. *Drop the long-real idea. A dollar trade would need new rules.*
- **D. Outside shock: U.S. rates or oil** — The U.S. two-year yield rises above 5.10%, or Brent falls sharply as Hormuz reopens. Emerging-market currencies tend to weaken together; cheaper oil removes a support. *No long-real position; exit if already in one.*

## The evidence, sorted by type

**Fact**
- Brazil voted on 4 October. With 99.5% of ballot boxes counted, Flávio Bolsonaro (PL) has 47.1% of valid votes and Lula (PT) 45.0%, 2.5 million votes apart. No one reached 50%, so the runoff is on 25 October. The other candidates (Cury, Santos, Caiado, Zema and others) took 7.8%.
- Final polls, valid votes: Datafolha (3 Oct) Lula 45%, Flávio 42%; Quaest (2–3 Oct) Lula 46%, Flávio 45%. Both showed a technical tie. Runoff scenarios (total votes), Lula–Flávio: Datafolha 47–46, Quaest 42–44.
- Neither central bank has met since 16 September: the Copom cut the Selic by 0.25 pp to 13.75% (unanimously, its 5th cut in a row and 1.25 pp since March) and the Fed raised its range by 0.25 pp to 3.75–4.00% (12–0). The policy-rate gap is 9.88 pp. Next: Fed 28 October, Copom 4 November.
- On 5 October the Ibovespa rose +7.7% to a record 206,912 points; the spot dollar closed at 5.0022. Late in the session the January 2028 DI stood at 12.66% (84 bp lower) and the January 2035 DI at 12.8% (133 bp lower). Brent fell 1.9% and Petrobras rose 8.2%. Press-reported figures from the live session; PTAX, used everywhere else, is the official average of four windows.
- For 2027 the government’s budget bill projects a R$18.6 bn primary surplus; the Senate’s Independent Fiscal Institution projects a R$86.1 bn deficit.

**Market pricing**
- USD/BRL (PTAX) closed at 4.9856 on 5 October: −4.55% against 5.2235 on 2 October, the last session before the vote, and the lowest close in the saved four months.
- The U.S. two-year Treasury yield was 4.84% on 5 October (4.83% on 2 October); the ten-year was 5.31%.
- Brazil’s fixed-rate curve (ANBIMA, 5 Oct): one year 12.68%, two years 12.60% (from 13.38% and 13.71% on 2 Oct), against 12.99% and 12.25% implied by economists’ own forecasts. Two-year implied inflation: 5.27% (from 5.91%).
- Brent: the EIA spot price was $113.96 on 29 September, below its $130.80 peak of 15 September but still above $100. The EIA series has not yet published the days after the vote.

**Survey & projections**
- Focus survey medians (2 October, answers collected before the vote): Selic 13.50% at end-2026, 12.00% at end-2027; inflation 5.0% in 2026, 4.3% in 2027; USD/BRL 5.20 at end-2026.
- Fed officials’ median projection: 4.1% at end-2026 (one more 0.25 pp rise), up from 3.8% in June.
- JPMorgan (28 Sep): after the election USD/BRL could trade from about 4.90, on a favourable fiscal signal even if incremental, to about 5.50, if the result is read as bad for fiscal consolidation. It saw the real near fair value, long-real positioning above past elections, options pricing about 6.5% of event moves across both rounds, a 9.5% real interest rate (against 6.6% in 2022) and gross debt of 83% of GDP (against 74%).

**My interpretation**
- Runoff arithmetic: holding turnout and blank votes constant, Flávio needs 37% of the 7.8% who voted for others, and Lula 63%. No candidate in that group is on the left, so I read this as favouring Flávio, without attaching a probability.
- First Mondays: after 2014, 2018 and 2022, USD/BRL fell between 2.9% and 3.8% on the first business day, and by the Friday before the runoff had given back 85% (2014) and 70% (2022) of it; in 2018 it extended it. On 5 October it fell 4.55%, the largest of seven elections. Three comparable cases are a base rate, not a test.
- Levels in the units of the move: entry (5.10) is 48% of Monday’s move given back, exit (5.22) 99% and the drop level (5.30) 132%. A 14-session move has a standard deviation of 2.1% (9.1% a year): the entry level sits 1.1 σ above today’s close and the drop level 2.9 σ above it.
