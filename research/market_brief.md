# Brazil macro: carry, the election and one conditional trade

*Research brief · thesis dated 5 October 2026. Educational research, not investment advice.*

## My current view — No position

Flávio Bolsonaro’s first-round lead is a surprise, not a verdict. In two of the last three elections the real gave back most of its post-vote rally before the runoff, so I stay out until 25 October and act only if prices confirm.

1. **The first round surprised.** Flávio Bolsonaro took 47.1% of valid votes to Lula’s 45.0%. The final Datafolha and Quaest polls had Lula ahead by 3 and 1 points: they missed the margin by 5.1 pp and 3.1 pp, in the same direction.
2. **The runoff is not a formality.** 7.8% of voters chose other candidates. To reach 50%, Flávio needs 37% of them and Lula 63%. No candidate in that group is on the left, so I read this as favouring Flávio, but it is not a forecast.
3. **The market has been here before.** USD/BRL fell between 2.9% and 3.8% on the first Monday after the 2014, 2018 and 2022 first rounds. By the runoff it had given back 85% (2014) and 70% (2022) of the move; in 2018 it kept falling. Long-real positioning was already above past elections (JPMorgan).

**What would make me act.** After the runoff, from 26 October: two daily PTAX closes below 5.10 with the U.S. two-year yield at or below 5.00%. I would read that as the market paying for a credible fiscal path, and paper-buy the real. Earlier closes, including any first-Monday rally, do not count.

**What would change my mind.** A PTAX close above 5.30 before any entry, Brazil’s two-year rate above 14%, or the U.S. two-year yield above 5.10%. Each would mean risk is rising, not falling.

## The paper trade: wait for the runoff, then buy the real only if the market confirms

Inactive, no position · earliest entry 26 October

- **Expression.** Long the Brazilian real against the U.S. dollar (short USD/BRL), tracked with the official PTAX rate. Paper only: no money, no execution.
- **Thesis.** If the election lowers the fiscal risk premium, the real should strengthen and stay stronger, because investors keep collecting a large interest-rate cushion while they wait.
- **Why it may not be priced.** The two-year rate sits 1.5 pp above the economists’ own path, and economists expect USD/BRL at 5.20 at year-end, so a durably stronger real is not the consensus. JPMorgan, though, sees the real near fair value with long positioning above past elections: some good news may already be priced.
- **Catalysts.** Runoff 25 October; Fed 28 October; Copom 4 November.
- **Entry.** From 26 October, the first PTAX after the runoff: two consecutive daily closes below 5.10, with the U.S. two-year yield at or below 5.00%. Earlier closes, including any rally on 5 October, do not count.
- **Drop the idea (before entry).** Any PTAX close above 5.30, higher than every close of the past four months (peak 5.2235 on 2 October). The idea is dropped, not reversed.
- **Exit (after entry).** Two consecutive closes above 5.22 (the top of the last 20 closes), or the U.S. two-year yield above 5.10%.
- **Review.** Around 4.94–5.00 (range low minus range width), or on 4 November at the latest. A review zone, not a target.

## What could happen before and after 25 October

- **A. Premium compresses after the runoff** — After 25 October, PTAX closes below 5.10 twice; the two-year rate falls a quarter point or more from 13.71%; the winner shows a credible fiscal path. Investors see less fiscal risk, and the carry cushion keeps attracting money. *Paper-buy the real if the U.S. two-year yield is at or below 5.00%.*
- **B. The first-round rally fades** — PTAX gives back most of its post-vote move before 25 October and is back above 5.22; polls tighten. The market doubts the first-round signal, as in 2014 and 2022. *No position. Wait for 25 October.*
- **C. Risk premium rises** — PTAX closes above 5.30; the two-year rate moves above 14%; implied inflation rises. Fiscal worries dominate; a weaker real plus expensive oil could make the Copom pause its cuts. *Drop the long-real idea. A dollar trade would need new rules.*
- **D. Outside shock: U.S. rates or oil** — The U.S. two-year yield rises above 5.10%, or Brent falls sharply as Hormuz reopens. Emerging-market currencies tend to weaken together; cheaper oil removes a support. *No long-real position; exit if already in one.*

## The evidence, sorted by type

**Fact**
- Brazil voted on 4 October. With 99.5% of ballot boxes counted, Flávio Bolsonaro (PL) has 47.1% of valid votes and Lula (PT) 45.0%, 2.5 million votes apart. No one reached 50%, so the runoff is on 25 October. The other candidates (Cury, Santos, Caiado, Zema and others) took 7.8%.
- Final polls, valid votes: Datafolha (3 Oct) Lula 45%, Flávio 42%; Quaest (2–3 Oct) Lula 46%, Flávio 45%. Both showed a technical tie. Runoff scenarios (total votes), Lula–Flávio: Datafolha 47–46, Quaest 42–44.
- Neither central bank has met since 16 September: the Copom cut the Selic by 0.25 pp to 13.75% (unanimously, its 5th cut in a row and 1.25 pp since March) and the Fed raised its range by 0.25 pp to 3.75–4.00% (12–0). The policy-rate gap is 9.88 pp. Next: Fed 28 October, Copom 4 November.
- For 2027 the government’s budget bill projects a R$18.6 bn primary surplus; the Senate’s Independent Fiscal Institution projects a R$86.1 bn deficit.

**Market pricing**
- USD/BRL (PTAX) closed at 5.2235 on 2 October, the last session before the vote: +0.9% since 24 September and the highest close in the saved four months (low: 5.0157).
- The U.S. two-year Treasury yield was 4.83% on 2 October (it was 4.87% on 24 September); the ten-year was 5.28%.
- Brazil’s fixed-rate curve (ANBIMA, 2 Oct): one year 13.38%, two years 13.71%, about 1.5 pp above the rate implied by economists’ own forecasts (12.25%). Inflation implied by the curve over two years: 5.9%.
- Brent: the EIA spot price was $113.96 on 29 September, below its $130.80 peak of 15 September but still above $100.

**Survey & projections**
- Focus survey medians (25 September): Selic 13.50% at end-2026, 12.00% at end-2027; inflation 5.0% in 2026, 4.3% in 2027; USD/BRL 5.20 at end-2026.
- Fed officials’ median projection: 4.1% at end-2026 (one more 0.25 pp rise), up from 3.8% in June.
- JPMorgan (28 Sep): after the election USD/BRL could trade from about 4.90, on a favourable fiscal signal even if incremental, to about 5.50, if the result is read as bad for fiscal consolidation. It saw the real near fair value, long-real positioning above past elections, options pricing about 6.5% of event moves across both rounds, a 9.5% real interest rate (against 6.6% in 2022) and gross debt of 83% of GDP (against 74%).

**My interpretation**
- Runoff arithmetic: holding turnout and blank votes constant, Flávio needs 37% of the 7.8% who voted for others, and Lula 63%. No candidate in that group is on the left, so I read this as favouring Flávio, without attaching a probability.
- Past first rounds: after 2014, 2018 and 2022, USD/BRL fell between 2.9% and 3.8% on the first business day. By the Friday before the runoff it had given back 85% (2014) and 70% (2022) of that move; in 2018 it extended it by a further 75% of that move. Three elections are a base rate, not a test, but they argue against paying for the first Monday.
- Levels against volatility: a 15-session move has a standard deviation of 2.2% (9% a year). Entry sits 1.1 σ below Friday’s close and the drop level 0.7 σ above it, while a typical first-round Monday moved 1.5 σ: these are triggers, not stop-losses.
